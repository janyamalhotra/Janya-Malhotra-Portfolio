PRODUCT DESIGN • CONVERSATION BOARD GAME

Talking Stage

Overview

In a world filled with online interactions and meet-ups, Talking Stage brings 2-5 players together in person. Players answer a series of personal questions as they move towards the finish; the first person to reach it wins.

Talking Stage is a conversation-driven board game designed to spark engaging conversations and help people get to know each other in a fun, natural, low-pressure way. Players can play on a first date, at a party, or with a group of friends when bored.

Project Type Product Design
Category Conversation Board Game
Timeline 2024 (2 weeks)
My Role Creative Lead & Product Designer
Project Format Group Project
Target Audience 16+
Tools Canva

Problem

Meeting people today is easy, but getting to know them genuinely is still hard. Most conversations stay on the surface, especially when people are meeting for the first time or trying to move past awkward small talk.

Online interactions often fail to build deep real-world rapport, while party environments lack low-stakes conversational structures that encourage curiosity and genuine vulnerability without putting players on the spot.

  • Surface-level friction: First dates and group meet-ups frequently stall at predictable small talk without meaningful emotional connection.
  • Digital disconnection: In-person social presence is easily eroded by phone checking when conversations lose momentum.
  • Psychological safety: Players need scaffolded escalation from lighthearted trivia to vulnerable questions to feel comfortable sharing openly.

Task and Constraints

The design task was to create an engaging physical board game with original cards and playful mechanics that make people talk openly, laugh, and find common ground. The primary constraints required balancing conversational intimacy with playful competitiveness, creating physical prototypes within a strict 2-week timeline, and ensuring clear card typography that is immediately readable across a table.

Game Summary

In a world filled with online interactions and meet-ups, Talking Stage brings 2-5 players together in person. Players answer a series of personal questions to move up the board towards the finish line; the first person to reach it is declared the winner.

Before the game begins, each player receives 2 Power Cards that can be played at any point. Players roll the die to advance across Question Tiles, Action Tiles, Dare Tiles, and Drowning Tiles, constantly navigating rewards and setbacks.

Rule Book

The game rules establish turn sequences, tile interactions, point systems, and edge cases (such as modified questions when a player does not meet a criterion). The tactile rulebook was designed with a high-contrast checkered border aesthetic for quick table reference.

1-Pointer Cards

1-Pointer Cards are low-pressure conversation starters designed to break the ice quickly without putting players on the spot. Example: “What’s one question you would ask a dog if they could talk?” Answering advances the player 1 step on the board.

2-Pointer Cards

2-Pointer Cards ask players to share opinions, lighter preferences, or playful dilemmas. Example: “Would you rather always say what you’re thinking on a date or hear everything your date is thinking?” Answering advances the player 2 steps.

3-Pointer Cards

3-Pointer Cards delve into deeper reflections, past relationships, and candid personal insights. Example: “What’s something you knew was over before you were ready to let it go?” Answering successfully advances the player 3 steps.

Dare Tiles

When landing on a Dare Tile, players must complete a quick physical or verbal challenge displayed on the board. Example: “Talk in a chosen accent for 3 minutes straight.” Failing to perform the dare requires moving back 4 steps.

Drowning Tiles

Drowning Tiles trigger high-stakes impromptu mini-games: Skip a Turn, Try Jinx, Echo Tile, Trivia, Ask Away, or Two Truths & a Lie. If the player fails their challenge, they move back 2 steps.

User Testing

Once the game was assembled, we played through it to understand how the mechanics worked in an actual social setting. The playtesting received very positive responses, with players enjoying the combination of conversation prompts, playful challenges and the competitive element of the board game. It helped us see how the game could encourage people to interact more naturally while keeping the experience engaging in its own right.

Ending Thoughts

Talking Stage demonstrated that intentional conversational mechanics can transform awkward social hesitation into authentic laughter and vulnerable connection.

"In a world increasingly mediated by screens, simple physical game structures can create the psychological safety needed to put our phones away and genuinely talk to each other."
